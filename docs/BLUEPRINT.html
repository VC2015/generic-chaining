Finite generic chaining in Lean

GPT-Sol 6.2, prompted by Victor Chernozhukov.

Suppose we use the largest of pp standardized estimation errors to construct simultaneous confidence bounds. If all the errors equal one standard Gaussian, adding another copy leaves the maximum unchanged. If they are independent standard Gaussians, the expected maximum grows on the order of logp\sqrt{\log p}. The two families have the same marginal distributions. What distinguishes them is the size of their increments. Generic chaining uses these increments to decide which coordinates can represent one another and how much each approximation costs.

Uses. simultaneous tail bound, tail integration.

Proof outline

At each level the number of distinct links is at most the product of the numbers of representatives at its endpoints. A union bound using the mixed-tail hypothesis therefore controls every link on that level. Summing the exceptional probabilities over levels gives, for v≥1v\ge1,

ℙ{maxt|Xt−Xa|>(5+v)B}≤2e−v1−e−v. \mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_t|X_t-X_a|>(5+v)B\right\} \le \frac{2e^{-v}}{1-e^{-v}}.

Indeed, whenever all links satisfy their bounds, their increments telescope from aa to tt for every tt. Integration gives the factor eight. The same estimate holds at B=0B=0 by taking a positive upper bound for the length and letting it decrease to zero.

Theorem 2

Finite Gaussian majorizing measures. For a Gaussian family, the standard deviation of an increment is its canonical distance. The next theorem determines, up to universal constants, how much this distance tells us about the expected maximum.

There exist CL,CU>0C_{\mathrm{L}},C_{\mathrm{U}}>0 such that for every r≥0r\ge0, every finite nonempty TT and every a:T→ℝra:T\to\mathbb{R}^r,

γ2(T,da)≤CLW(a),W(a)≤CUγ2(T,da). \gamma_2(T,d_a)\le C_{\mathrm{L}} W(a),\qquad W(a)\le C_{\mathrm{U}}\gamma_2(T,d_a).

One may take CU=100C_{\mathrm{U}}=100 and CL=K*/cgC_{\mathrm{L}}=K_*/c_{\mathrm{g}}, where cgc_{\mathrm{g}} and K*K_* are the constants used in Lemma 5 and Lemma 6.

The two directions have separate proofs. The bound on the width uses Theorem 1. The bound on the net cost uses Lemma 5 and Lemma 6.

Lean. GenericChaining.gaussianWidth_mono_of_increment_le

Uses. comparison in orthogonal coordinate blocks.

Proof outline

Put the two families in orthogonal Gaussian coordinate blocks, replace the maximum by the smooth function β−1log∑iexp(βxi)\beta^{-1}\log\sum_i\exp(\beta x_i), and interpolate between the families. Gaussian integration by parts expresses the derivative of the expected smooth maximum as a sum of differences of increment variances with nonnegative weights. The hypothesis orders those variances. Integration along the interpolation and passage to the limit β→∞\beta\to\infty give the comparison. See also van Handel, Theorem 6.8.

Lean. GenericChaining.gamma2_le_of_localGrowth_exact

Uses. greedy partition bound, cost of cutting the partition tree.

Proof outline

Within each cell, choose a remaining center whose small ball has largest FF and remove a larger ball around it. Repeating this operation orders the children. A child chosen late has many earlier competitors, and the growth assumption bounds its rank in terms of the decrease in FF further down the branch. Summing over intervals of three levels makes these decreases telescope. To see how the rank bound controls the number of representatives, assign a child of rank j≥1j\ge1 the weight 1/(4j2)1/(4j^2) and multiply the weights along each branch. At a given threshold only a bounded number of cells can have products above the threshold. Choosing representatives from these cells produces nets with the cardinalities in the net definition. The two-point case of growth controls the cost of the first net.

Application to the rectangle CLT

For a centered bounded sum WW with unit coordinate variances, use both signs of each coordinate and include zero as a base point. Bernstein’s inequality supplies the two increment distances in Theorem 1. The net-cost direction of Theorem 2 controls the variance distance by the Gaussian width. Lemma 7 controls the discrete envelope distance. If ZZ has the same covariance as WW, these estimates give the rectangle paper’s bound

𝔼∥W∥∞≤C{𝔼∥Z∥∞+δlog(ep)}. \mathbb E\|W\|_\infty\le C\{\mathbb E\|Z\|_\infty+\delta\log(ep)\}.

The chaining inputs are proved in this repository. The signed-coordinate Bernstein calculation and the base-point estimate appear in Lemma C.2 of the rectangle paper.
