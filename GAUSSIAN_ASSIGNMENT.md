The rectangle CLT uses the expected Gaussian maximum to pay for an admissible net. Our main deliverable is the following dimension-free lower bound on that maximum. Let $T$ be finite and nonempty, let $a_t\in\mathbb R^r$ for $t\in T$, and let $G$ have the standard Gaussian law on $\mathbb R^r$. Set $d(s,t)=\|a_s-a_t\|_2$ and $W(T)=\mathbb E\max_{t\in T}\langle G,a_t\rangle$. Prove in Lean that there is a universal $C>0$ such that
$$
\gamma_2(T,d)\le C\,W(T).
$$
Quantify the constant before the dimension, index set, and vectors. Use the repository's existing admissible-net functional. The maximum is one-sided. Allow repeated vectors, singular covariance, and arbitrary common translations. A singleton has width and chaining cost zero.

1. **Connect Gaussian width to the repository's geometry.** Use a concrete standard Gaussian law, derive the canonical increment identity, and justify measurability and integrability of finite maxima. Reuse the existing surjective pullback and zero-contrast lemmas for duplicated points. If using real-valued costs at the public interface, prove the finite-net cost is finite before converting the extended nonnegative functional to a real number.
2. **Prove growth of local Gaussian width.** Follow the finite specialization of van Handel's proof. For separated centers and nonempty subsets in balls of sufficiently small relative radius, prove that the width of their union exceeds the smallest local width by a universal multiple of the separation times the square root of the logarithm of the number of centers. State the separation and radius conventions explicitly. Check the pinned libraries for finite Sudakov minoration and Gaussian concentration for Lipschitz local suprema. Import or prove the precise estimates used. Discharge those hypotheses for the concrete Gaussian process.
3. **Construct the admissible nets.** Formalize the deterministic passage from growth to labelled partitions and then to the admissible cardinalities in this repository. Prove the branchwise cost estimate, including the singleton first level, and choose one representative per cell. Combine this result with Gaussian growth to obtain the displayed theorem. An abstract partition lemma assuming growth is a useful intermediate result. The final Gaussian theorem must discharge growth.
4. **Complete the converse and export both directions.** Prove $W(T)\le C'\gamma_2(T,d)$ using the checked Dirksen theorem with exponential metric zero and Gaussian increment tails. If its statement includes a base-point increment term, bound it by a universal multiple of the diameter and use $\operatorname{diam}(T)\le2\gamma_2(T,d)$ from the singleton first net. Export the two directions separately and together. The lower bound in step 3 has priority for the rectangle paper.
